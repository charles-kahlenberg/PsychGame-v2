# Phase 2

Notes from the 2026-11-04 design meeting (54 min recording + whiteboards).
Nothing here is final. The next step is a storyboard for the team to pick apart.

## Goal

Same purpose as Psych 101: show students how the scientific method is used to
study all of human thought and behavior, from sensation to social life to
psychological disorders. Phase 2 has to stop feeling like an assignment
("you feel like you're doing an assignment when you do the scenario") and avoid
"chocolate-covered broccoli" (do the worksheet, then get the fun part).

## Story: a shrouded figure who discovers themselves

- The player starts as a **shrouded, gender-neutral figure**, a black silhouette.
  Each act reveals more of who is underneath. References: *Journey*, *Inscription*
  and *Blue Prince* (a story inside a story), *Project Hail Mary*.
- **Intro scene:** "Who are you?" Pick a gender, then answer a few personality
  questions (brave? kind? down on it all?). The answers decide what the figure
  finally looks like once it's fully revealed.
- **Self-reflection milestones:** "Here are all the concept cards you've met so
  far. Pick three and explain how they relate to you." Later on: "How do these
  two concepts work together in your life?" Doing these reveals more of the
  character. ("We're all interested in ourselves.")
- **Other personas:** some scenarios could put you in someone else's shoes, e.g.
  playing as a two-year-old for the early-childhood cards, or as a baby duck
  imprinting.
- **Inner world** (whiteboard sketch): a barren island with a flag that grows as
  you build yourself up.
- **Ghost / astral-plane idea:** this was pitched as a way to justify wildly
  different scenarios. The concern is that it drifts from "scientific study of"
  into the supernatural. It is not decided.

## Structure: four acts, one per exam

Most Psych 101 courses have four exams, so there are **four acts, each ending in
a milestone or boss** that players hit before the matching exam.

| Act | Units | Theme |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Overview, research methods, brain and nervous system, sensation and perception | Build self (physical self) |
| 2 | Learning, memory, development | Build self (the brain) |
| 3 | Motivation and emotion, problem solving, language, social psych | Build up others |
| 4 | Psychological disorders + misc. | Build up others |

- The first half builds yourself. The second half uses what you built (plus new
  cards) to help others understand themselves, so it can be harder. Reference:
  *Endacopia* (Andyland), where helping other people is how you discover who
  you are.
- **Final challenge idea:** you've built who you are, then a crisis hits (a
  disorder, something that throws you off). How well you set yourself up decides
  whether you have the tools to handle it. "That's the point of therapy."
- **Difficulty comes from cumulative effects, not a steeper curve**, as in
  *This War of Mine*, *60 Seconds* and *Frostpunk*: what you do early on shapes
  the middle, and what you do in the middle shapes the end. Here it's a positive
  version: the more you pick up early, the better you do at milestones.

## Gameplay: deck building

- **Starter deck:** about 20 cards at the start of each chapter.
- **Each successful scenario offers a choice of card(s) to add**, so players
  build toward a niche deck.
- **Passing a chapter or boss unlocks the next chapter's starter deck.**
- **Deck types** follow the chapters/areas (research methods, learning,
  cognitive, social, biological). Each type has its own special effect, like
  *Magic*. Types unlock over time. They make the player stronger, and skipping
  them makes the game harder. This replaces weapons and combat ("fighting is not
  everybody's cup of tea").
- Rewards should come throughout play, not only at bosses. Historical
  psychology studies or figures could act as tools or helper items.
- **Boss levels:** cards with no hints. Other pitches were an "epiphany" or
  moral-question moment, and drawing one unknown card at a time while writing
  the narrative. Not decided.

## Cost of failure and scoring

- **Problem:** today you move on no matter how you score, so a player can type
  junk, learn nothing, and still collect every card.
- **The game can't be resource-loss or roguelite-style,** because it runs across
  a whole semester. You only ever gain cards.
- **Proposed fix: a skillometer**, from the cognitive math tutors. It shows a bar
  per skill that rises as you do well (a simple Bayesian estimate that you know
  the skill). A skill gets a check mark at 95%. Fill all the bars to advance.
  Strong players do few scenarios and weak players get more practice, so the
  quickest way through is to do well. (Whiteboard: "skill bar?")
- **Needs a scenario pool:** about a dozen per unit, and players won't see all of them.
- **Scoring needs rework.** The grading prompt was tuned on GPT-3.5 and feels
  worse since the move to GPT-4 (players are yelling at the screen). It also
  shows raw "40/100"-style scores. Note: grading now runs on `gpt-4.1-mini`
  through the psych-ai worker. Non-programmers can help iterate on the prompt.
- **Exams:** for transfer to show up, exams need questions that test it. The goal
  is at least one open-ended question per exam. The professors will ask fall
  instructors whether they'd adjust their assessments.
